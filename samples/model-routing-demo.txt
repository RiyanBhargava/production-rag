TRADING PLATFORM CUSTOMER OPERATIONS
Fictional internal handbook for a document-search demonstration. These are invented procedures, not real company policies, trading advice, or regulatory requirements. All times and thresholds are illustrative. This handbook covers account checks, payments, customer communication, incidents, and handovers. Each named section describes a separate operational topic so answers can cite different passages from the same document.

WITHDRAWAL COMPLAINT ACKNOWLEDGEMENT
Support must acknowledge a withdrawal complaint within 15 minutes of receiving it. The acknowledgement confirms receipt; it is not a promise that money will be released within 15 minutes. Record the complaint time, withdrawal reference, customer concern, and next update time in the case. The acknowledgement deadline applies to both standard withdrawals and security-flagged withdrawals. If investigation is still pending, send a progress update every 2 hours during staffed support hours, even when there is no new result. Keep acknowledgement and resolution timestamps separate. A complaint about delay does not itself change the withdrawal's processing status. The assigned support agent owns customer updates until another agent explicitly accepts the handover.

STANDARD WITHDRAWAL REVIEW
A standard withdrawal is reviewed within 24 hours after account verification is complete. The review clock starts after verification, not when an unverified customer first requests payment. Support checks the account verification status, withdrawal reference, payment method, and current processing status before giving an estimate. If a standard withdrawal review exceeds 24 hours after verification, support escalates the case to payments operations with the withdrawal reference and elapsed review time. Do not open multiple payment requests to try to speed up one withdrawal. Payments operations confirms whether the request is pending, rejected, or completed. Support communicates that confirmed status and records it in the case. The 24-hour review target is not a guarantee of bank settlement time.

SECURITY FLAGGED WITHDRAWAL
A security-flagged withdrawal remains on hold until the security team clears it. Support must not release the withdrawal, remove the security hold, or bypass account verification. Support escalates a security-flagged withdrawal to security operations and records the case reference. Only security operations may confirm clearance of the hold; a payments queue update does not constitute security clearance. After clearance, payments operations handles the normal withdrawal review. Do not promise a release date while the security hold is active. Give the customer a status update without revealing internal fraud-detection rules, alert thresholds, or investigative methods. Record the time of the security referral and the team that owns the next action.

URGENT CUSTOMER COMMUNICATION
Customer urgency does not override a security hold or account verification requirements. An urgent request can raise the priority of a status check, but it cannot authorize withdrawal release. Acknowledge the customer's concern, describe the outstanding verification or review in plain language, and state when the next update will arrive. Never say that a withdrawal is approved if the recorded status is on hold. For a security-flagged withdrawal, explain that an account review is pending without describing fraud-detection rules. If verification is incomplete and a security hold is active, support requests the missing verification through the secure workflow and refers the hold to security operations. Both checks must be resolved; completing one does not automatically satisfy the other.

ACCOUNT VERIFICATION WORKFLOW
Account verification requires an accepted identity document and a completed address check in this fictional workflow. Support can view the verification status and identify which check is outstanding, but cannot approve documents on behalf of the verification team. Customers submit documents through the secure account portal, not in public chat or an agent's personal email. If an uploaded document is unreadable, request a replacement through the portal and explain the quality issue. Verification operations reviews a complete resubmission within 1 business day. Record the submission reference rather than copying identity-document images into a support note. An incomplete verification status means the standard withdrawal review clock has not started.

PAYMENT METHOD OWNERSHIP
Withdrawals must use a payment method recorded as belonging to the verified account holder. A mismatch between the account name and payment-method owner goes to payments operations for review. Support must not instruct the customer to use a friend's card or a third party's wallet as a workaround. Where a method is unavailable, payments operations selects an approved alternative and records its reason. Support explains that approved step without inventing a replacement payment route. Check the method's status before requesting that the customer repeat a payment. A completed withdrawal reference must not be reused for a new request. Record any approved alternative against the original case to keep the investigation history together.

MISSING DEPOSIT INVESTIGATION
For a missing deposit, support first checks the deposit reference, amount, payment method, transaction time, and account activity. If the provider has marked a deposit successful but it is absent from account activity for more than 60 minutes, escalate to payments operations. Ask for a provider transaction reference through the secure case channel; never ask for a full card number or card security code. Support must not manually credit the account based only on a customer screenshot. Payments operations reconciles the provider record with the internal payment record and decides the next action. A pending provider transaction is described as pending, not as a failed or completed deposit. Store the investigation outcome and its supporting reference in the case.

DUPLICATE PAYMENT CLAIMS
When a customer reports two charges for one intended deposit, support collects both transaction references and checks whether the records represent two completed payments or one payment plus a temporary authorization. Support must not promise a refund before payments operations confirms the result. Send both references, amounts, and transaction times for reconciliation. If one entry is a temporary authorization, explain that its release is controlled by the payment provider and avoid inventing a deadline. If both are completed, payments operations chooses the approved correction. Link related complaints to one primary case while retaining each reference. The customer receives one coordinated status update rather than conflicting messages from separate tickets.

FAILED WITHDRAWAL RECONCILIATION
If a withdrawal is marked failed, payments operations checks whether the balance was restored and whether the provider recorded any settlement. Support checks the status before suggesting another withdrawal request. Do not ask the customer to repeat a request while settlement is unknown, because that can create a duplicate-payment investigation. If the balance has not been restored within 30 minutes of a confirmed failure, escalate the reconciliation case to payments operations. Include the failure reference and observed balance status, not a guessed explanation. Support distinguishes a confirmed failure from a temporary pending status. Once the correction is confirmed, record its reference and explain the available next step to the customer.

COMPLAINT CASE RECORD
Every complaint record includes the received time, acknowledgement time, customer concern, relevant transaction references, current owner, action history, and promised next update. Support separates the customer's allegation from verified findings. A support note should say which team confirmed a payment or security status and when it was checked. Do not overwrite an earlier note when the status changes; add a dated update. A complaint is closed only after the outcome has been communicated and any open follow-up has an owner. If the customer disputes the outcome, reopen the case and preserve the previous decision history. Complaint records must not contain passwords, one-time codes, full card numbers, or copied identity-document images.

ESCALATION OWNERSHIP
Payments operations owns payment reconciliation, overdue standard withdrawal reviews, and missing-deposit investigations. Security operations owns active security holds and suspected account compromise. Verification operations owns identity and address checks. Support owns acknowledgement, clear explanations, and scheduled customer updates. A case involving both incomplete verification and a security hold needs actions from both verification operations and security operations; support coordinates their references in one case. Sending an escalation does not remove support's communication responsibility. The receiving team must accept ownership or identify the correct team. If no team accepts an urgent case within 30 minutes, the support shift lead assigns an owner and records the decision.

SUSPECTED ACCOUNT COMPROMISE
If a customer reports an unfamiliar login or an unauthorized account change, support creates a security incident case and escalates immediately to security operations. Use the secure recovery workflow to check identity; never ask the customer to disclose a password or one-time authentication code. Support must not remove a security restriction simply because the customer is upset or in a hurry. Security operations determines protective actions and confirms when recovery checks are complete. Record the reported event time, affected activity, and incident reference without unnecessary sensitive information. For any withdrawal linked to the incident, preserve the active hold until security operations explicitly clears it.

PLATFORM OUTAGE TRIAGE
A suspected platform outage is escalated to platform operations when at least 3 independent customer reports describe the same failure within 10 minutes. Support records the affected feature, first observed time, and example error message. Platform operations confirms incident severity and publishes an approved status notice. Support must not label an issue a confirmed outage before the incident team confirms it. Check whether a payment complaint is actually a platform access problem before sending it to payments operations. Once an incident is confirmed, link affected support cases to the incident reference instead of creating separate engineering investigations. Do not promise a restoration time that the incident team has not supplied.

OUTAGE CUSTOMER UPDATES
During a confirmed platform outage, the incident communication owner publishes a customer status update every 30 minutes until recovery is confirmed. Support uses the approved incident message and directs customers to the status notice. Distinguish degraded access from missing funds; a login failure alone does not establish a payment loss. If no restoration estimate exists, say that investigation continues and provide the next update time. An outage message must not reveal infrastructure credentials or unapproved root-cause claims. After recovery, platform operations confirms the affected feature is available before support sends a resolved message. Payment cases with outstanding reconciliation remain open even when platform access has recovered.

TRADING EXECUTION QUESTIONS
When a customer questions an execution result, support gathers the order reference, instrument, displayed time, and the customer's description of the issue. Market operations reviews execution records and provides the approved explanation. Support does not offer personalized trading recommendations or promise that a disputed trade will be reversed. Separate an execution investigation from a payment investigation: an order reference is not a withdrawal reference. If the customer provides a screenshot, keep its timestamp and context, but do not treat it alone as a complete execution record. Record the reviewed outcome and source team. Where platform access was disrupted at the same time, link the execution case to the confirmed incident reference.

PRIVACY AND SAFE SUPPORT NOTES
Support records only the information needed to investigate the case. Use masked payment details and secure case references in ordinary notes. Never request passwords, one-time authentication codes, full card numbers, or card security codes. Identity-document files stay in the approved verification portal. When escalating, share the account and transaction references needed by the receiving team rather than copying unrelated personal data. Customer messages must not expose fraud-detection thresholds, internal access tokens, or another customer's activity. If sensitive information is accidentally pasted into a case, notify the privacy owner and follow the approved redaction workflow while preserving an audit entry describing the correction.

SHIFT HANDOVER CHECKLIST
At the end of a shift, the outgoing support agent hands over open cases with the current owner, outstanding verification or security actions, relevant references, and next customer update time. The incoming agent explicitly accepts the handover before the outgoing agent leaves the case. Highlight withdrawal complaints approaching their acknowledgement deadline and investigations whose next update is due. Include any pending payments, security, verification, or incident-team response. A handover must not reset the original complaint time or processing clock. The incoming agent reads the latest confirmed status before contacting the customer. The shift lead assigns any case that has no accepting owner.

QUALITY REVIEW AND MEASURES
The support quality reviewer checks a weekly sample of 20 closed cases. Review whether acknowledgements met the deadline, updates were sent as promised, escalations reached the correct team, and notes distinguished verified findings from assumptions. Check that security holds and verification requirements were respected, even for urgent customers. The reviewer also checks whether the final message matched the recorded outcome and whether sensitive information was excluded from ordinary notes. A failed quality check leads to coaching and a tracked corrective action; it does not authorize changing payment or security decisions. Operational measures include acknowledgement time, overdue standard reviews, update timeliness, and unresolved handovers.
